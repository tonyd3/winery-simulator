# Ten more grapes

The catalog contains 34 varieties and 64 research projects (30 techniques and 34 individual grape studies). Each addition can be planted in any region after its study, used as a breeding parent, fermented, stored, blended, bottled, and sold. New estates still begin with two founding grapes.

These are base study costs and durations, excluding prerequisite projects. Home-region favorites cost 20% less and take two fewer study weeks; an operating research lab accelerates progress. A game year contains 12 weeks. Planting and breeding are separate purchases.

| Gamay | Vine science | $4,250 | 70 | 12 | Pinot Noir; powerful reds can overwhelm it |
| Carménère | Experimental viticulture | $22,350 | 230 | 32 | Merlot or Cabernet Sauvignon |

| Albariño | Experimental viticulture | $19,950 | 230 | 32 | Vermentino |

Gamay joins Burgundy’s regional favorites, Petite Sirah joins Napa’s, Graciano joins Rioja’s, and the three Rhône whites join Barossa’s. These favorites receive the existing planting, growth, harvest-quality, and home-region research advantages. Other varieties can still suit a region’s climate without being a regional favorite.

The additions offer different tradeoffs. Gamay grows best in cool conditions and has generous yields; Graciano trades crop size for finesse. Roussanne has low resilience and yield but high finesse, making care more valuable. All soil preferences, heat/resilience/finesse values, yields, and prices are simplified game balance, not agronomic measurements.

Pairing adjustments remain bounded between −6 and +4. Syrah–Viognier is proportion-sensitive: Viognier supports the blend at up to 15%, then gradually becomes overpowering. Tiny additions have small effects. This recipe still requires **Varietal assemblage** and **Red & white experiments**. Hybrid recipes inherit pairing tendencies through their parentage. Good pairings cannot guarantee a 90-point bottle from ordinary fruit; already scored reserves and released bottles retain their scores.

Existing saves keep their earned grape access and active studies. Collection rewards from saves predating individual research stay limited to the original 24 varieties, including unfinished legacy collection projects. The ten additions require individual studies in those estates too. Save version six and the browser storage key remain unchanged.

## Inspiration

Regional identities and some pairings draw on these primary sources. Additional pairing choices are gameplay experiments; the numerical bonuses and proportion thresholds are design choices.

- [Bourgogne Wine Board: Bourgogne Passe-Tout-Grains](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/bourgogne-and-its-appellations/gallery_files/site/321/402/79294/79349.pdf) — Pinot Noir and Gamay pairing.
- [Rioja: Graciano](https://riojawine.com/en-us/the-designation/grape-varieties/graciano/) — its complementary role with Tempranillo.
- [Napa Valley Vintners: wine varieties](https://napavalley.wine/wine) — Petite Sirah as a distinct grape from Syrah.
- [Wines of Chile: winegrowing regions](https://www.winesofchile.org/winegrowing-regions/) — Carménère’s Chilean regional identity.
- [Rías Baixas: geography and terroir](https://www.riasbaixaswines.com/about/geography-terroir/) — Albariño’s Atlantic setting.
- [Austrian Wine: Grüner Veltliner tasting guide](https://www.austrianwine.com/fileadmin/user_upload/PDF/AVZs/WG15_GV_Verkostung-WEB.pdf) — aromatic character.
- [Rhône Valley: grape varieties](https://www.vins-rhone.com/en/rhone-valley-vineyards/grape-varieties) and [Rhône Rangers: white grapes](https://www.rhonerangers.org/wine-education/white-grapes) — the white-grape family and complementary textures.
- [Langmeil: Three Gardens](https://www.langmeilwinery.com.au/products/2025-three-gardens-viognier-marsanne-roussanne) — a Barossa producer’s Viognier–Marsanne–Roussanne blend.
