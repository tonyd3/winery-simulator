# Grape library

The catalog contains 64 varieties and 97 research projects (33 techniques and 64 individual grape studies). Each addition can be planted in any region after its study, used as a breeding parent, fermented, stored, blended, bottled, and sold. New estates still begin with two founding grapes.

These are base study costs and durations, excluding prerequisite projects. Home-region favorites cost 20% less and take two fewer study weeks; an operating research lab accelerates progress. A game year contains 12 weeks. Planting and breeding are separate purchases.

## Iberian, Greek, Georgian, and South African additions

Twelve more varieties expand the library: seven reds and five whites. Bobal and Verdejo offer lower-cost studies and generous crops; Touriga Nacional, Saperavi, and Assyrtiko trade yield and study expense for finesse. Moschofilero favors a light, aromatic style, while Baga needs more careful tending and patient maturation. Their numeric traits and pairings are game design choices.

## Italian and French additions

Ten additions explore Italian wine traditions: Aglianico, Sagrantino, Corvina, Montepulciano, Nero d’Avola, Dolcetto, Fiano, Verdicchio, Garganega, and Arneis. Eight explore French wine traditions: Tannat, Carignan, Cinsault / Cinsaut, Pinot Meunier, Savagnin, Melon de Bourgogne, Clairette, and Grenache Blanc. These selections describe regional associations, not exclusive countries of origin.

Sagrantino and Aglianico trade crop size and study cost for finesse and a powerful red style. Cinsault, Dolcetto, and Melon offer less expensive studies and larger crops. Fiano and Savagnin offer higher finesse with smaller white-grape harvests. All numerical traits remain game balance. The existing eight regions retain their founding grapes and regional favorites; these additions can be studied and planted in any of them, with local climate and soil affecting results.

Each grape has its own sensory profile and at least one complementary pairing. Recipe ideas include traditional combinations and invented cellar experiments; they do not impose appellation rules. Powerful new reds still clash with Pinot Noir and Gamay. Pinot Meunier uses the existing still-red-wine model; it does not introduce sparkling wine. Savagnin does not automatically produce oxidative or vin jaune flavors. Grenache Blanc is a separate white-grape entry; Cinsault and Cinsaut share one study. Search accepts either straight or curly apostrophes in Nero d’Avola.

Gamay joins Burgundy’s regional favorites, Petite Sirah joins Napa’s, Graciano joins Rioja’s, and the three Rhône whites join Barossa’s. These favorites receive the existing planting, growth, harvest-quality, and home-region research advantages. Other varieties can still suit a region’s climate without being a regional favorite.

The additions offer different tradeoffs. Gamay grows best in cool conditions and has generous yields; Graciano trades crop size for finesse. Roussanne has low resilience and yield but high finesse, making care more valuable. All soil preferences, heat/resilience/finesse values, yields, and prices are simplified game balance, not agronomic measurements.

Pairing adjustments remain bounded between −6 and +4. Syrah–Viognier is proportion-sensitive: Viognier supports the blend at up to 15%, then gradually becomes overpowering. Tiny additions have small effects. This recipe still requires **Red & white experiments**, reached through **Advanced blending**. Hybrid recipes inherit pairing tendencies through their parentage. Good pairings cannot guarantee a 90-point bottle from ordinary fruit; already scored reserves and released bottles retain their scores.

Existing saves keep their earned grape access and active studies. Estates with access to the previous 34-grape catalog still need individual studies for all 18 Italian and French additions. Collection rewards from saves predating individual research stay limited to the original 24 varieties, including unfinished legacy collection projects. All 40 later additions require individual studies in those estates too. Save version six and the browser storage key remain unchanged.

## Inspiration

Regional identities and some pairings draw on these primary sources. Additional pairing choices are gameplay experiments; the numerical bonuses and proportion thresholds are design choices.

- [Irpinia wine consortium](https://consorziovinidirpinia.it/vini/irpinia-doc-vino/) — Aglianico and Fiano in Campania.
- [Montefalco: Sagrantino](https://www.consorziomontefalco.it/montefalco-sagrantino-docg/) — its Umbrian identity.
- [Valpolicella: grape varieties](https://www.consorziovalpolicella.it/en/the-varieties/) and [Soave: the grapes](https://www.ilsoave.com/en/the-grapes/) — Corvina and Garganega.
- [Abruzzo wine consortium](https://www.vinidabruzzo.it/consorzio/?lang=en) and [Wines of Sicily: meet the grapes](https://winesofsicily.com/meet-the-grapes/) — Montepulciano and Nero d’Avola.
- [Langhe: Dolcetto](https://www.langhevini.it/en/i-vitigni-del-territorio/dolcetto/) and [Roero wine types](https://www.consorziodelroero.it/tipologie/) — the Piedmont selections.
- [Comité Champagne: grape varieties](https://www.champagne.fr/en/about-champagne/a-great-blended-wine/champagne-and-its-grape-varieties) — Meunier’s regional identity; sparkling production is outside the game’s current model.
- [Loire: grape varieties](https://www.vinsdeloire.fr/fr/cepages?page=0), [Jura wine guide](https://www.jura-vins.com/medias/documents-a-telecharger/docs-utiles/VDJ-guide2015new-V2-Francais.pdf), and [Plantgrape: Tannat](https://www.plantgrape.fr/en/varieties/fruit-varieties/268/export) — Melon, Savagnin, and Tannat.

- [Bourgogne Wine Board: Bourgogne Passe-Tout-Grains](https://www.bourgogne-wines.com/wine-and-terroir/bourgogne-and-its-appellations/gallery_files/site/321/402/79294/79349.pdf) — Pinot Noir and Gamay pairing.
- [Rioja: Graciano](https://riojawine.com/en-us/the-designation/grape-varieties/graciano/) — its complementary role with Tempranillo.
- [Napa Valley Vintners: wine varieties](https://napavalley.wine/wine) — Petite Sirah as a distinct grape from Syrah.
- [Wines of Chile: winegrowing regions](https://www.winesofchile.org/winegrowing-regions/) — Carménère’s Chilean regional identity.
- [Rías Baixas: geography and terroir](https://www.riasbaixaswines.com/about/geography-terroir/) — Albariño’s Atlantic setting.
- [Austrian Wine: Grüner Veltliner tasting guide](https://www.austrianwine.com/fileadmin/user_upload/PDF/AVZs/WG15_GV_Verkostung-WEB.pdf) — aromatic character.
- [Rhône Valley: grape varieties](https://www.vins-rhone.com/en/rhone-valley-vineyards/grape-varieties) and [Rhône Rangers: white grapes](https://www.rhonerangers.org/wine-education/white-grapes) — the white-grape family and complementary textures.
- [Langmeil: Three Gardens](https://www.langmeilwinery.com.au/products/2025-three-gardens-viognier-marsanne-roussanne) — a Barossa producer’s Viognier–Marsanne–Roussanne blend.
